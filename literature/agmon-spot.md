# Deconstructing Amazon EC2 Spot Instance Pricing

Agmon Ben-Yehuda, Ben-Yehuda, Schuster, Tsafrir, ACM Transactions on Economics and Computation, 2013 (preliminary version IEEE CloudCom 2011). Evidence from search snippets (Technion portal, ACM record, DCD interview); full paper not opened.

- Claim: EC2 spot prices were not set by supply and demand; they were usually drawn from a tight interval consistent with a random reserve price (a hidden price below which bids are ignored), generated by an algorithm.
- Implication per the authors: published spot prices reveal little about real client bids, which questions studies treating them as market bids.
- Later (DCD interview, secondary): the authors say the 2017 pricing change ended the market system; an NSF-hosted paper describes the post-2017 mechanism as an undisclosed dynamic algorithm.

Relevance to us: administered cloud spot series are best modelled as an algorithmic process (bounded noise around a reserve), not a market price; a 2013 result about EC2, not about GPU rental markets.
