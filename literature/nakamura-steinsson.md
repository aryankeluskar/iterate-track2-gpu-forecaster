# Five Facts about Prices: A Reevaluation of Menu Cost Models

Nakamura and Steinsson, Quarterly Journal of Economics 123(4), 2008. Read from fetched PDF text (abstract and Section on hazard functions).

- Median frequency of non-sale price change for consumer prices is 9 to 12 percent per month (identical items), against 19 to 20 percent including sales; finished-goods producer prices are comparable to consumer prices excluding sales. A time-weighted average of implied regular-price duration is about 7.5 months (8.7 months excluding substitutions).
- About one third of non-sale price changes are decreases. The frequency of increases covaries strongly with inflation; the frequency of decreases and the sizes of changes do not.
- Hazard function of a price change versus time since the last change: no evidence of upward-sloping hazards for individual products. Excluding sales and for producer prices the hazard is slightly downward sloping for the first few months and then mostly flat, with a large spike at twelve months for services and producer prices. Hazards including sales are steeply downward sloping where sales are frequent. They control for heterogeneity with Meyer (1990).
- Frequency of price change is highly seasonal (highest in Q1). Benchmark menu-cost model fits facts 1 to 3 but not 4 and 5 (seasonality, flat hazard).

Relevance to us: time since last change is a weak predictor beyond the first months, so a constant-hazard (geometric waiting time) is a reasonable default for step series; calendar effects (start of quarter, month) and the inflation/market-wide shock matter more. These are consumer and producer goods, not GPU rate cards, so transfer is an assumption to test.
